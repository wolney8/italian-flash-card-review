# Italian for Beginners deck schema — v5.4

This document supplements the v5.3 schema and records the refinements agreed after daily review.

## Conversation prompts

Conversation cards must be reusable and independent of a particular class date, tutor or learner.

Preferred format:

1. **Instruction:** `Say this in Italian:`
2. **Target:** the natural English phrase.
3. **Clue:** the exact verb, pronoun, register or construction being practised.

Examples:

- `Say this in Italian: “What are you called?” Use chiamarsi and informal tu.`
- `Say this in Italian: “See you on Tuesday.” Use the farewell pattern a + day; no verb is written in the Italian phrase.`

Avoid vague prompts, dangling names, lesson-date instructions and duplicated question/help text.

## Age with avere

Age cards should test the specific Lesson 4 decision: choosing the form of **avere** that agrees with the subject.

Preferred recall side:

- show the Italian sentence with one blank;
- show `ho · hai · ha · abbiamo · avete · hanno` as static visual choices;
- place the natural English translation underneath.

Preferred answer side:

- show the complete Italian sentence;
- break down every meaningful unit in visual word tiles;
- explain why the chosen form of **avere** matches the subject;
- distinguish literal Italian structure (`have + years`) from natural English (`be … years old`).

For example:

- `I miei figli` = my children, plural subject;
- `hanno` = they have, third-person plural of **avere**;
- `sette e dieci` = seven and ten;
- `anni` = years.

## Update safety

- Keep existing note IDs, GUIDs, card IDs and website card keys.
- Update the existing card rather than creating a duplicate.
- Existing Google Sheet comments remain linked to the same stable card key.
- Do not change unrelated cards merely because a new version is being issued.
