# Article progression: Lessons 2 and 4

This document defines how plural definite and partitive/indefinite articles should be divided across Lessons 2 and 4 without duplicating the same learning task.

## Source basis

### Lesson 2 - introduction

The Lesson 2 notes introduce grammatical gender, singular/plural noun endings, and the complete article families:

- definite: `il / lo / l' / la` -> `i / gli / le`
- indefinite singular: `un / uno / un' / una`
- plural `some`: `dei / degli / delle`

Lesson 2 therefore legitimately contains first-exposure cards for all of these forms.

### Lesson 4 - detailed plural application

The Lesson 4 handout revisits plural formation and explicitly links plural articles to gender/number concordance and sentence building. Its table gives detailed rules and examples for:

- `i / gli / le` = the
- `dei / degli / delle` = some
- masculine ordinary consonant, vowel/special start, and feminine plural patterns
- full noun phrases such as `i maestri / dei maestri`, `gli amici / degli amici`, and `le maestre / delle maestre`

Lesson 4 therefore owns the rule application and contrast practice.

## Pedagogical split

### Lesson 2: recognise and map the article family

Cards should test one small decision:

- recognise `i`, `gli`, or `le` as plural `the`
- recognise `dei`, `degli`, or `delle` as plural `some`
- connect the pairs:
  - `i` -> `dei`
  - `gli` -> `degli`
  - `le` -> `delle`

The answer side should introduce the literal construction:

- `dei = di + i`
- `degli = di + gli`
- `delle = di + le`

It must explain that `di + article` can structurally mean `of the`, while in these cards the fused form is functioning as a partitive/indefinite plural meaning `some`.

Lesson 2 should not carry the full Lesson 4 table or long sentence-agreement tasks.

### Lesson 4: apply the detailed rules

Cards should require the learner to complete real plural noun phrases:

- `___ maestri`
- `___ amici`
- `___ maestre`

They should contrast:

- definite quantity: `i maestri` = the teachers
- unspecified quantity: `dei maestri` = some teachers

The answer should explain:

- gender
- plural number
- starting sound
- the definite/partitive pair
- the `di + article` construction where relevant

## Terminology

Use Laura's course terminology:

> Articoli Indeterminativi / Partitivi Plurali

For learner clarity, describe `dei / degli / delle` as **partitive forms functioning as an indefinite plural meaning “some”**. They are not simple mechanical plurals of `un / uno / una / un'`.

## Source and clarification boundary

Laura's Lesson 4 table groups `S + consonant`, `P + consonant`, and `Z`. The deck may retain a clearly labelled added-study clarification that not every word beginning with `p + consonant` takes the special article family; the practical special starts include forms such as `ps-` and `pn-`, while words such as `professore`, `problema`, and `prato` use the ordinary family.
