Articles

Choose the meaning, then check the noun’s gender, number and first sound.

When to use each type

The

A particular person or thing the listener can identify.

Il libro sul tavolo.
The book on the table.

Definite article

A or an

One countable person or thing, introduced without specifying which.

Compro un libro.
I’m buying a book.

Indefinite article

Some

An unspecified amount, or several people or things.

Compro del pane e dei libri.
I’m buying some bread and some books.

Partitive article

A and an are singular. For an unspecified plural, you can use some, as in un libro → dei libri.

What does s + consonant mean?

Look at the letter straight after the s. In studente, it is t, a consonant. In specchio, it is p.

s + consonant
lo studente · uno studente
lo specchio · uno specchio

s + vowel
il signore · un signore
il sapone · un sapone

The lo / uno pattern applies to masculine nouns. Feminine scuola uses la scuola, una scuola, le scuole.

Also use this masculine pattern for z: lo zio · uno zio · gli zii · degli zii.

Less common beginnings

gn uses lo gnomo; ps uses lo psicologo; x uses lo xilofono. Consonantal i / y, sounding like the y in “yes”, also uses this pattern: lo yogurt.

pn traditionally uses lo pneumatico. Everyday il pneumatico is also accepted. P + consonant is too broad a rule: il professore and un professore use the ordinary pattern.

A silent h follows the next sound: l’hotel · un hotel. Other borrowed words can vary with pronunciation.

Some

For these partitive forms, join di to the definite article.

Apostrophes and useful reminders
  • Choose from the Italian noun. English “a” or “an” does not choose the Italian article: uno zio means “an uncle”.
  • Un amico is masculine. Keep the space after un.
  • Un’amica is feminine. It is the shortened form of una amica. The apostrophe replaces the final a.
  • L’amico → gli amici. L’amica → le amiche. The noun’s gender determines the plural.
  • Check the word immediately after the article. Compare lo studente with il bravo studente.
  • The same forms can mean of the in another sentence. Il colore del libro means “the colour of the book”.
  • Italian also uses the definite article for general categories: Mi piace la musica means “I like music”.
What about “in the”, “on the” and “from the”?

These are preposition + article combinations, called preposizioni articolate. For example, in + il → nel, su + il → sul and da + il → dal.

The “in, on and from” exercises cover these combinations. This guide focuses on choosing the article.
